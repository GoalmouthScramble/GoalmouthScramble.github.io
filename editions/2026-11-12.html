Midweek round-up

Enfield back on top

Three days after losing top spot, Enfield Town (1st) took it back. They beat Lewes (5th) 2-0 on Tuesday, while Welling United (2nd) let a lead slip and drew 2-2 at Whitehawk (21st). The top two are now level on 37 points, with Enfield ahead on goal difference and a game in hand. Eastbourne Borough (3rd) bounced back from their Dartford defeat by beating Dulwich Hamlet (14th) 4-2, which leaves them three points off the top, also with a game in hand on Welling.

Lewes's defeat let Dartford (4th) past them without kicking a ball. Leatherhead (8th) won at Chatham Town (10th) for a third straight league win, and they now sit a point outside the play-offs with games in hand on most of the clubs around them. At the bottom, Cray Wanderers (17th) beat Three Bridges (20th) two days after their FA Cup tie, and that climb out of the bottom four pushed Brentwood Town (19th) back into it.

Top story

Sam Youngs's 400th

On Saturday, Sam Youngs (FWD) had a penalty saved as Enfield Town (1st) lost at home to AFC Whyteleafe (13th) and dropped off the top. On Tuesday he started on the bench against Lewes (5th), and when he came on after 57 minutes it was his 400th appearance for the club. Nobody else has ever reached that number for Enfield.

The game was goalless when he arrived. Within 16 minutes he had scored one and set up the other, Enfield had won 2-0, and with Welling dropping points at Whitehawk they finished the night back on top. He is now five goals short of Liam Hope's club scoring record.

Good week, bad week, crazy week

Good week

Josh Rees

Eastbourne Borough (3rd)

Handed the captain's armband for Dulwich's visit, he watched his side go a goal down after six minutes. Then, before half-time, he scored twice: first he took a touch towards goal and dispatched the ball into the bottom-left corner, and then he slotted home at the third attempt after two blocked shots on a counter-attack. As a result, Eastbourne answered Saturday's 4-1 defeat at Dartford, and he moved up to nine league goals, joint second in the Golden Boot.

Bad week

Olly Davis

Enfield Town (1st)

With Sam Youngs on the bench, he took Enfield's first-half penalty against Lewes (5th), only for it to clip the bar and fly over. He said afterwards that he slipped on the greasy surface as he struck it. It was Enfield's second missed penalty in four days, and he was substituted a minute after Youngs came on to win the game.

Crazy week

Sammie McLeod

Cray Wanderers (17th)

On Sunday he opened the scoring against Stockport County in front of a sell-out crowd and the BBC cameras, and he played the full 120 minutes as Cray lost 3-2 after extra time. Just over 48 hours later he played all 90 against Three Bridges (20th) in teeming rain, in the away kit, at a game with free entry, and his through ball set up the opener. Cray won 2-0 and climbed out of the relegation places.

Enfield Town (1st)2Lewes (5th)0

Enfield were without two players on international duty, and they dominated a first half they could easily have won. Amrit Bansal-McNulty was brought down by Tyler Cox for a penalty, but Olly Davis slipped and clipped the bar, and Sonny Hart's header was ruled not to have crossed the line. Then Sam Youngs came off the bench for his 400th Enfield appearance, had a goal ruled out for offside, and after 69 minutes swivelled to send a powerful shot in off a deflection. Four minutes later he squared for Bansal-McNulty, who rounded Louis Rogers and just got the ball over the line. It completed a league double over Lewes, who lost 4-0 at home to Enfield on the opening day.

In other news

Shirts off their backs

After the Stockport tie, Cray Wanderers (17th) gave their home kit commemorative status and let the players keep their shirts. That left no time to replace them before Tuesday, so Cray played Three Bridges (20th) in claret. Entry was free, the rain started at about 6pm, and 512 came along, compared with the 2,500 who had packed in on Sunday.

The six pound fifty test

Leatherhead's (8th) match reporter now judges away grounds by the price of chips, a Bovril and a Mars bar. Chatham Town (10th) did it for £6.50, which beat Brentwood and Farnham Town by 50p. In the closing stages the home side were even briefly credited with an equaliser on Flashscore, although the ball had actually hit the side netting.

Whyteleafe do it again

Four days after winning at Enfield, AFC Whyteleafe (13th) knocked Dartford (4th) out of the Velocity Cup on Wednesday. Whyteleafe twice came from behind, and Palace Francis, who scored the opener at Enfield, equalised six minutes from time to make it 2-2. They then won the shoot-out 3-0.

Home again

Liam Nash had come off the bench to score against his old club Dartford (4th) on Saturday. On Tuesday Steve King gave him his first start since September, and he had a hand in Eastbourne's (3rd) equaliser before scoring the fourth himself.
